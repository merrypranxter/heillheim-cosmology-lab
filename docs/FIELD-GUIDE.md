# Field guide

## What lives where
- `sources/`: metadata about the original recordings and external sources; no video binaries
- `analysis/`: sourced theme dossiers, interpretation and clarification tracking
- `cosmology/`: the Atlas's catalog of places, forces, entities and causal mechanics
- `chronology/`: order of statements, proposed events and later confirmations or revisions
- `ghost/`: ready-to-use study packets and reusable analysis instructions
- `atlas-expansions/`: contributor-authored proposals
- `data/`: machine-readable claims and Atlas nodes
- `templates/`: repeatable entry forms

## Intake pipeline
**CAPTURE → IDENTIFY → TRANSCRIBE → CHECK → ATOMIZE → MAP → QUESTION → EXPAND**

1. Assign the next `REC-` ID and record the source URL, edit history and capture date.
2. Work from original-speed audio whenever possible. Mark caption/OCR uncertainties explicitly.
3. Break source statements into atomic claims with timestamps.
4. Put interpretive connections in Atlas Readings, not in the creator's mouth.
5. Connect entities and propositions by their IDs, preserving dated revisions.
6. Record questions that could resolve ambiguities; welcome creator corrections.
7. Only then develop expansions, crediting their contributors.

## Confidence notation
- **A / audio-verified:** checked against clearly intelligible original speech
- **B / source-visible:** clearly visible source text or reliable creator-posted caption
- **C / provisional:** accelerated captions, noisy OCR, paraphrase, or secondhand episode descriptions
- **U / unreviewed:** source has not yet been transcribed

Confidence measures the reliability of **attribution**, not the truth of the underlying claim.

## An ideal atom
`CLAIM-002-03 — REC-002, 02:27–02:40 (accelerated edit), confidence C — Atlas describes certain neurotransmitters as modulators of magical access.`

## Before publishing
Ask: did we make a claim about a person rather than their source statement? Did we confuse a proposed cause with a verified event? Is this a public source? Would someone who disagrees still be fairly represented?
