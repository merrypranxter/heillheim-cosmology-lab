# Bioelectricity, cellular restoration and Sonic Bone Therapy

**Source anchors:** REC-010 / CLAIM-010-01/02/03 (published explainer and cut-off ASR); REC-007 / CLAIM-007-01/02 (reported grub interaction; recording boundary unsettled); REC-040 / CLAIM-040-01 (Timeline 14 sonic-bone concept).

## Strongest documented connection: BrainEx and OrganEx
A landmark [BrainEx study (Vrselja et al., Nature 2019)](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6844189/) [EXT-003] reported maintained or restored **microcirculation, metabolism and selected cellular responses** in isolated pig brains four hours after death. The work explicitly describes an **absence of global electrocorticographic activity**; it did not demonstrate consciousness or restoration of personal identity.

[OrganEx (Andrijevic et al., Nature 2022)](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41586-022-05016-1) [EXT-004] extended a perfusion/cytoprotective approach to pig bodies after one hour of ischemia, preserving tissue and restoring selected cellular processes across multiple organs. This is crucial research on ischemic injury and organ preservation; it **did not restore a conscious, functioning whole organism**.

**Bioelectric developmental signaling** is a real research field examining membrane potential and tissue patterning. These findings do not identify an independently verified transferable conscious “resurrection signal.” The creator's account and the published explainer's attempt to bridge cellular repair and consciousness must remain separate attributed propositions.

## The distinct concept of Sonic Bone Therapy
REC-040 describes material allegedly learned from Timeline 14: acoustic/mechanical stimulation targeting long bones and marrow to raise stem-cell production. Neighbouring research includes osteocyte mechanotransduction, exercise loading, ultrasound tissue effects, stem-cell mobilization and transplantation. None establishes **unlimited marrow stem cells** or an intervention with a proven ability to cure leukemia or reverse ageing.

In fact, [BMJ's 2017 clinical guideline](https://www.bmj.com/content/356/bmj.j576) [additional checked source] recommended against routine low-intensity pulsed ultrasound fracture healing based on patient-relevant outcomes; [NICE](https://www.nice.org.uk/about/what-we-do/research-and-development/research-recommendations/ipg622/1) found research-only evidence for a particular high-risk nonhealing context. That concerns a different clinical indication and **does not constitute a trial of the Atlas's claimed Sonic Bone Therapy**.
