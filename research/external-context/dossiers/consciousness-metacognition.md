## Source-stated framework
The creator calls magic “the science of consciousness” and connects access to neurotransmitter priorities and social context. He links attacks on safety or belonging with diminished freedom to explore higher consciousness. REC-055 gives a two-stage metacognitive method: first disentangle received reactions from learned conditioning; then inspect what a speaker wants an audience to think, feel and do. REC-050 separately outlines a music-evoked reminiscence and screen-free presleep dream-restoration sequence.

## Outside neuroscience and psychology
**Serotonin, dopamine, oxytocin and endogenous opioids** influence complex overlapping learning, stress, reward, bonding, mood and attention systems. Their roles are not reducible to universal one-neurotransmitter switches. No reproducible mainstream study establishes the precise four-neurotransmitter formula as a gate for psionic capacity.

**Maslow's hierarchy** is a useful descriptive model for human needs and motivations, but an inflexible universal tier sequence lacks robust support. Chronic insecurity and stress can influence attention and cognitive demands, though effect sizes, mechanisms and replication differ among studies. Separately, the Creator's political analysis about civil-rights restrictions is a stated interpretation; simply noting genuine stress effects does not prove a coordinated physiological weapon.

**Metacognition** includes observing one's own judgments and confidence. **Decentering** and **cognitive defusion** reduce identification with initial thoughts by treating them as mental events. The source's two-stage training has a recognizable psychological analogue; an additional reported grid-access effect would require its own evidence. See [decentering literature review](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5103165/) (source lead).

**Autobiographical memory and music** are useful neighbours for the dream exercise. A playlist can be an evocative retrieval cue; nightly media reduction might change attention and sleep conditions. These analogies do not demonstrate specific recovered memories or universal outcomes.
