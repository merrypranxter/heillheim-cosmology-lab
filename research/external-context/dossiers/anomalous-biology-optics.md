# Anomalous biology, iridescent hair and thermal control

**Source anchors:** REC-011 / CLAIM-011-01/04/08; REC-023 / CLAIM-023-02; REC-015 / CLAIM-015-01; REC-029 / CLAIM-029-01; REC-039 / CLAIM-039-01. **Hair anatomy claims beyond the creator's introduction appear in a published explainer** and need their own attribution.

## Optics: distinct mechanisms
- **Iridescence:** reflected/scattered colour changes with angle because of microstructure and interference.
- **Fluorescence:** excitation by incoming wavelengths followed by light emission at other wavelengths; does not require angle-specific surface reflection.
- **Phosphorescence:** delayed emission persists after excitation, subject to lifetimes and material.
- **Biological waveguiding:** photonic transport through particular structures is a separate measured property, not implied solely by coloured hair.

Hair cuticle, cortex, medulla, melanin and other chromophores determine appearance. A vivid UV shift may motivate investigation of pigments, residues, fluorescence and geometrical surface effects without classifying it as a neural antenna. The proposed “ninth optical organ” appears clearly in Gemini's expanded synopsis, but the existing claims chiefly describe a published explainer's unusual keratin and electrical assertions; exact **ninth-organ wording needs original-media confirmation**.

## Comparative anatomy
Some vertebrates possess pineal/parietal photoreceptive structures that respond to light and participate in biological timing; these are not evidence for an unrecognized human follicle-based eye. Normal human hair follicles have sensory innervation, but innervation by itself is not photoreception.

## Thermoregulation
Sweating, shivering, vasoconstriction, vasodilation, brown-adipose thermogenesis and learned behavioural adaptation are established. REC-015/029 describe extreme thermoregulation. A claimed exposure cannot be evaluated without duration, ambient conditions, wind, skin and core temperatures, hydration, tissue effects and independent repeated measures.

## Research questions
What was the illuminant's spectrum? Does colour shift track viewing angle or illumination change? Can ordinary hair products and environmental contaminants be excluded? Is any neural response to light reproducible? These are *questions for historical documentation*, not an invitation to unsafe experimentation or extreme exposure.
